# Changelog

%% Newest first. Record significant design decisions and which pages changed. %%

## 2026-09-24
- Touch controls hardened after review: a stick is owned by the touch id rather than the finger slot (a replanted thumb kept the old centre and the ship drifted); the rebuild guard watches the screen size rather than the reach it produces, which could never fire on a rotation; a thumb adopted after a pause or a rebuild is taken up where it is rather than where it landed, so resting through a pause no longer resumes as a full push; the half a thumb landed in decides its stick for the touch's whole life; losing the touchscreen lets go instead of freezing; and touch now merges with keyboard, mouse and pad per channel, so a right thumb firing no longer zeroes the steering. Raised two design questions: iOS has no way to pause on purpose, and auto-rotation is on in a side-scroller. Pages updated: Open Questions.
- Touch controls: two floating virtual sticks, each centring wherever the thumb lands rather than sitting in a fixed corner, because a thumb cannot find a fixed spot without looking and screens differ in size and hand. Left moves, right aims and fires while held. Touch has no separate fire-forward trigger, so a right thumb held still fires straight ahead and pushing it past the dead zone aims: it goes through the same rule the gamepad uses, so the dead zone and the firing arc have one answer rather than a second one written for touch. The sticks are drawn only while a thumb is down and smaller than the thumb's reach, to say where the stick centred without covering the fight. Pages updated: Controls.
- Pausing on mobile is the system Back button, which Unity reports as Escape, so it is the same control as the desktop's rather than a second one. **No on-screen pause button**, which the design had previously called for: a button in the corner is permanent clutter on the screen the player is trying to read. Back on Android minimises the app rather than quitting it, so it is a navigation control and nothing more; Quit stays hidden on mobile and WebGL, where ending the application is the platform's job.
- Losing focus now pauses a level, on every platform: alt-tabbing, taking a call or switching app means the player has stopped playing, and a ship that dies while they are away is not a fair death. Coming back does not unpause. Never in the editor, where focus belongs to the Console and the Inspector in turn and an automated test run never has it at all: without that carve-out every PlayMode test would have paused the game and then timed out waiting for a game that was not running.
- Fixed before anybody played it: the knob showing how hard a stick is pushed moved by the canvas scale factor less than the thumb actually had, because an offset already measured in screen pixels was scaled again. Found by a test that drives the real sticks in the real scene and asserts where the parts land; a screenshot had looked plausible and proved nothing, because photographing the canvas changes the very coordinate space the bug lived in.

- Players have accounts. The pilot name is the account: one name to remember, shown on the boards, with a password beside it, so a score belongs to the player rather than to the machine they were sitting at and follows them to any device. Two people sharing a machine keep their own scores. Signing up and signing in are separate buttons rather than one that guesses, and nobody is made to have an account at all: the same screen offers playing without one, and that choice is remembered rather than asked again every session. The service's rules for a name and a password are checked before the game calls it, so a mistake comes back at once and in the player's words. Replaces the anonymous device sign-in, under which a reinstall lost every score and a shared machine shared one identity. Pages updated: Scoring, UI Flow and Screens.
- Fixed: the game asked for a pilot name but never created anything, so it was still identifying players by device. Reported from play, not caught by a test.
- Fixed: resuming a session asked the service to sign in even when somebody already was, which it refuses, and the refusal was reported as "not signed in". Anything that signed in before the boards were first needed hit it.
- Fixed: an entry on a board was marked as the player's own by comparing player ids without checking that the player's own id was known, so when it was not, a stranger's run could be highlighted as theirs.

## 2026-09-23
- Leaderboards, end to end: six Unity Gaming Services boards, one per mode and difficulty, created in the project's environment and reachable from the title without playing. A run is submitted when it is over, at a game over in either mode or at clearing the campaign's last level, never at an intermediate Sector Clear, and a run worth nothing is never sent. The pilot's name is asked once on the way into the first run and lives in Settings afterwards. A player with no network loses nothing: their own best runs are kept on this machine and shown instead, said plainly, and their results screen simply shows no placing. Pages updated: Scoring, UI Flow and Screens.
- Fixed before anyone played it: nothing ever signed the player in, so the online boards could not have worked at all. Every submission failed with "the boards could not be reached" and every board quietly fell back to the local one, which is indistinguishable from having no network. The service is signed into the first time a board is actually needed. Found while looking at the screens rather than by a test, because the one test that talks to the real service signs in itself.
- Review fixes, all of them the same shape (the failure is invisible because the fallback looks exactly like the ordinary offline case): a sign-in already in flight turned away everyone who asked during it, so pressing a button while the board screen loaded claimed the boards were unreachable on a working connection, for good; a space in the player's name made every online submission fail, because the service refuses a name containing whitespace and the rename shared its error handling with the score; local scores were kept as floats, so an eight-figure Endless score came back a few points from the one just shown; every row of the local board was painted in the colour that exists to pick the player's own run out; back from the difficulty screen re-asked the name the player had just given; and the placing line appearing a network round trip after the results panel moved Retry and Title up the screen while the player was reaching for them.
- Boss art left the sprite atlas. Measured first: the prefabs themselves are only 27 KB and their pooled instances another 27 KB, so unloading them would have saved nothing, but the atlas page they shared was 10.71 MB and the eight boss bodies were two thirds of its content. An atlas page loads whole, so one Dart on screen kept every boss's art resident. The bodies now live in `Resources/Bosses/`, are named by `BossDefinition.SpritePath`, and are loaded when a boss arrives and freed when the level ends or an Endless cycle turns. The atlas repacked itself to 1024x1024, and the art a campaign level holds fell from about 13.3 MB to 5.8 MB as the Editor reports it (a player build compresses further, so the ratio is the result rather than the absolute figures). The eight bodies are 1.31 MB together as BC7 and a fight holds one. The cost is batching: the boss draws in the same sorting band as the enemies, so it splits that batch. Pages updated: Art Direction, Technical Design, Open Questions.
- The game now loads only the art it is using. The backdrops and the Endless skies were referenced directly from the level data, which hands the decision to Unity: the dependency graph behind one scene was the whole game's, and when it loaded depended on the platform. They live under `Resources/` now and are held by path: a level takes its own backdrop and gives it back when it ends, and an Endless run holds the sky it is showing plus the one arriving for the length of a dissolve, two of six rather than six. The next sky is fetched in the background shortly before its dissolve rather than loaded at the moment it appears, so nothing blocks mid-fight. Two tests guard it: one drives a full lap of the ring and fails if anything accumulates, the other asks the engine whether the freed texture is still loaded (Unity's destroyed-object null does not report an unloaded asset, so it cannot answer that question). Each backdrop is 1.13 MB resident (DXT1), so the set is 15.8 MB and a run now holds 1.13 MB of it. Boss and enemy prefabs are still held by reference and are the next candidates. Pages updated: Technical Design, Open Questions.

## 2026-09-22
- One scene for the whole game. There were ten: a title, eight near-identical copies for the campaign levels and one for Endless, kept in step by a pile of editor scripts that regenerated them, which meant a change to any level scene was lost on the next build run. A level is now data: `Campaign.asset` holds the eight `LevelDefinition` assets in play order, the runner picks the one the run has reached, and the backdrop comes from the definition rather than from whatever sprite a scene was saved with. Endless became a mode rather than a scene. Adding a level means making a definition and adding it to the list: no scene, no build-settings entry, no builder. Seven editor scripts deleted (about 1,900 lines), including every one that generated a scene; the title and level scenes are now ordinary assets edited in the Editor. Pages updated: Levels, the eight level pages, UI Flow and Screens, Technical Design, Open Questions.
- Endless skies: the backdrop used to cut to the next level's the instant a boss died, which announced the loop and broke the run's continuity. A run now drifts through a ring of six skies of its own, each held then dissolved into the next and the last leading back to the first, on a slow clock deliberately unrelated to the waves, the cycles and the boss. The skies are painted for blending: soft, dark, no focal point, matched in composition and value (their brightness was normalised so the ring cannot pulse as it drifts). A fade of zero is rejected by the rules, so the sky cannot cut again. Pages updated: Art Direction, Core Loop.
- Fixed: the Fullscreen setting had the same fault as the volumes, found by review rather than by playing. The flag saying "the saved value has been applied once this session" was never reset, and this project runs with domain reloading off, so from the second play session onward the game adopted the window's current state instead of applying the saved one, writing the player's choice away in the process. The flag is reset per session now, and the editor's own window is never adopted. Pages updated: UI Flow and Screens.
- Fixed: the saved settings did not take effect when the game opened. The music and effects volumes were loaded correctly but never reached the mixer, so the game started at full volume and stayed there until the player opened the Settings screen and nudged a slider, which is what made it look like nothing was saved. Unity's audio system discards the first write of a session, made as the title scene wakes; the game now reads the groups back and writes again whenever they disagree with the settings. Pages updated: Audio Direction.

## 2026-09-20
- Levels 2 to 8, with their seven bosses: the campaign is now playable end to end. Each level is a scene generated from Level 1 with its own AI backdrop and wave script (11 to 13 waves), introducing enemies on the level the GDD says. The bosses are one shared behaviour driven by data rather than seven classes: health, how long the core stays shut, and a table of attacks scoped to a phase and a band of remaining health. That table is how a boss escalates and how the Overmind's three phases work. New boss moves: hurled meteors, sweeping beams, a dash across the screen, turret volleys and a gravity pull that drags the ship. Pages updated: Levels, Wave System, Asset List, Scope and Milestones, Open Questions, and a new page per level.
- Fixed: the title screen had lost its music and its UI sounds. Rebuilding the title scene starts from an empty scene, so it had discarded the feedback objects another builder had added, and nothing noticed. The title builder now restores them itself, and a test checks that every playable scene can be heard and starts the right track.
- Endless code review: the per-cycle speed escalation reached nothing, because every spawn site still used the difficulty's settings rather than the cycle's, so only the crowd grew; the "Reached cycle N" line had no label to write into, and neither did the HUD's cycle counter, both being serialised references that no builder created; hazards left over from a finished cycle were decrementing the new cycle's wave counts, which could clear a wave early; boss music never returned to the level theme; and the menu and feedback builders skipped the Endless scene, which was exactly the drift that copying it from Level 1 exists to prevent. Pages updated: Open Questions.
- Endless mode: cycles of ten waves and a boss, each cycle faster, fuller and worth more, ending only when the player runs out of lives. It authors no content of its own: a cycle draws its waves at random from the campaign levels up to its own number plus one, so it opens at about the campaign's difficulty and is unpredictable after that, and its boss is any of the eight but never twice running. One scene, no Sector Clear between cycles, the sky changing each time; the HUD counts the cycle and the results screen reports how far the run got. The difficulty screen's Endless entry became a switch, since Endless needs a difficulty of its own. Pages updated: Core Loop, UI Flow and Screens, Scope and Milestones.
- Boss damage split: a shot into a boss's armoured hull is now worth half its damage instead of nothing, and a shot into the open core is worth full. Both fractions are per boss, in its definition, and a closed core counts as a hull hit rather than being thrown away. A boss fight was previously a wait for the core to open; shooting the boss anywhere is now worth doing, with the core as the reward for aiming. Pages updated: Levels, Hive Carrier.
- Fixed: the menu actions are one shared project asset, so a scene on its way out disabled the input of the scene arriving; after a level change the player could have been left with no way to pause. The enables are reference counted now, and a test walks between scenes checking the router is still listening.
- Boss and level code review: every boss's core hitbox was a fraction of the core drawn on screen (the collider shares the transform that is scaled to fit the sprite), so shots that looked like hits went through the Frost Lancer 40 per cent of the time; the Frost Lancer's second dash could never happen, because a dash took longer than the phase that started it, and the attack was dropped for good rather than retried; an attack whose health band opened mid-phase fired twice in consecutive steps, which is fourteen bullets 20 ms apart; a sweeping beam only looked for a target on its damage clock, so it swept through the player more often than it burned them. Bosses also now hold their fire until they have flown in, rather than opening the fight from off screen. Pages updated: Levels, Level 03, Level 04, Wave System.
- Content pass, the six remaining enemies: Swarm drone, Gunship, Diver, Mine Layer, Frigate and Sniper, with their proximity mines and the Sniper's beam. Each behaviour is an engine-free rule (holding pattern, dive, front shield, beam cycle, mine) driven by a thin view; their definitions, prefabs and hitboxes are generated by a re-runnable editor script, and their art was generated to the GDD's world sizes. A mine can be shot down for 50 points. They are not yet in any level: levels 2 to 8 come next. Pages updated: Enemies and Hazards, Scoring, Asset List, Open Questions.
- Enemy code review: the Sniper's beam took its aim when the warning ended, which made it a guaranteed hit and its warning a lie; it now commits to the line as the warning begins, and the line is drawn from that moment and goes out between shots. Every enemy prefab was wearing the template Dart's hitbox and muzzle, so a Frigate three times its size was hit only across a narrow band: both are now built from each ship's own art, and a test checks it. Divers entering from beyond the right edge no longer despawn before they arrive, difficulty now scales charges, mines and warnings as well as guns, and mines no longer all detonate the moment the last player is out. Pages updated: Enemies and Hazards, Open Questions.
- M4, campaign skeleton: a campaign is now one run through the levels listed in the campaign asset. Lives, health and weapon carry forward, score and kills accumulate, Sector Clear leads to the next level, and clearing the last one shows the campaign ending. Progress is saved per difficulty and finishing the campaign sets the Endless unlock (the mode itself is still to come). Pages updated: Core Loop, UI Flow and Screens, Scope and Milestones.
- M3 completed: three AI-generated music tracks (menu, Level 1, boss) that crossfade on scene changes and when the boss warning sounds, and an audio mixer with Music and SFX groups whose volumes both Settings sliders now drive, in decibels. Pages updated: Audio Direction, Scope and Milestones, Open Questions.
- M3 code review: the level had no audio listener, so every sound played into silence; effects now use a URP material rather than a legacy one, draw above the ship and its shield, and are pooled up front; the hit flash brightens a named sprite instead of tinting towards white (which was invisible on near-white art) and can no longer whiten the boss's core or the player's shield; a closed boss core no longer sounds as though it took damage; meteors flash and their size decides the explosion; the effects volume is applied once and reaches sounds already playing; a long sound is no longer cut off by the shots over it; menu clicks follow the button pressed rather than the screen appearing. Pages updated: Art Direction, Audio Direction, Technical Design.
- M3 (first pass), game feel: pooled particle effects (small, large and multi-stage boss explosions, muzzle flash, pickup sparkle, shield ripple), a white hit flash on anything damaged, and screen shake on hits, lost lives and boss deaths, honouring the Settings toggle. Sixteen AI-generated sound effects cover every entry in the audio table, played through rate-limited voices with per-sound trim and slight pitch variation. Music and the mixer groups remain. Pages updated: Art Direction, Audio Direction, Scope and Milestones.
- M2, game flow and menus: Title scene (Play, Settings, Credits, Quit) with difficulty select, in-level pause, settings (music, effects, screen shake, fullscreen) saved between sessions, credits, and Game Over and Sector Clear results screens with buttons. Menus are built by re-runnable editor scripts; the menu colour palette is recorded. Pages updated: UI Flow and Screens, Controls, Art Direction, Technical Design, Scope and Milestones.
- Fixed: the title drew over its tagline on the first frame of the game, and corrected itself only after another screen was opened and closed. The menu builders now lay each screen out before saving the scene, so the saved positions are final, rather than relying on a layout pass that a screen already visible at load never triggers.
- M2 code review: the pause menu can no longer open in the beat between a run ending and its results appearing; menu selection survives a background click, so keyboard and gamepad never go dead; settings are written to disk when a screen closes rather than on every frame of a slider drag; the game no longer fights the player over the fullscreen window; the scene builders no longer save and close a level the user had open. Pages updated: Controls, Technical Design, Open Questions.
- Firing limited to the forward arc (35 degrees either way) to match the ship's tilt: 360-degree aiming was top-down behaviour, wrong for a side-scroller. Pages updated: Controls, Mechanics.
- Wave pacing tightened: first wave after 1 s (was 2 s), 1 s between a cleared wave and the next (was 3 s), and a wave can hold the level for at most 10 s (was 20 s). New rule: the player is never left waiting on an empty screen. Pages updated: Wave System.
- Firing arc code review: the arc has a single source of truth (the rules constant, no longer a per-prefab tilt setting), so the ship's nose and its shots cannot drift apart; firing with no aim shoots straight ahead; the spread's overshoot outside the arc is now an open question. Pages updated: Controls, Open Questions.
